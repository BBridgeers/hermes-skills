## Voice & Tone Rules (HARD CONSTRAINTS)
1. **Confident, never arrogant.** State facts. Let numbers speak.
2. **Specific > generic.** Every claim ties to a real role, metric, or outcome.
3. **Conversational professional.** Read it aloud — if it sounds like a robot wrote it, rewrite it. Blake speaks like a real person who happens to be great at his job.
4. **No filler phrases**: Ban "I am writing to express my strong interest", "I believe I would be a great fit", "passionate about", "results-driven professional". Replace with specific, earned statements.
5. **Open with substance.** First sentence should demonstrate knowledge of the company/role, not just generic enthusiasm.
6. **Close with action.** End with what Blake will do in the role, not what he hopes to get from it.
7. **Mirror the job description's language** — if they say "drive adoption", use "drive adoption". If they say "stakeholder engagement", use "stakeholder engagement". But weave it naturally.

## Anti-Patterns (NEVER DO THESE)
- ❌ "I am excited to apply for..."
- ❌ "I believe my skills align perfectly with..."
- ❌ "As a results-driven professional..."
- ❌ "I am passionate about..."
- ❌ Generic praise of the company without specifics
- ❌ Listing skills without tying them to outcomes
- ❌ Using "synergy", "leverage", "paradigm"
- ❌ Starting every paragraph with "I"
- ❌ Mentioning the career gap apologetically — frame it as intentional
- ❌ "Additionally," / "Furthermore," / "Moreover," transition soup
- ❌ "Not just X, it's Y" parallelism
- ❌ Rule-of-three lists: "A, B, and C" without real substance
- ❌ "At its core", "The real question is...", "Let's explore..."
- ❌ "stands as" / "serves as" / "functions as" / "represents" — use "is/are/has"
- ❌ "-ing" fake depth phrases: "highlighting...", "underscoring...", "reflecting..."
- ❌ Em dashes (—) used more than once per letter
- ❌ Significance inflation: "pivotal", "testament to", "crucial role", "turning point"
