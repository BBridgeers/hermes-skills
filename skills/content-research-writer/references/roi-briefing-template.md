# ROI / Business Case Document Template

Use this structure when building a client-facing business case or ROI briefing document — the kind that proves financial value with cited data, not sales rhetoric.

## Required Tone

McKinsey-deliverable style: rigorous, data-backed, declarative. Every claim has a source. The document proves, it does not persuade. Avoid exclamation points, superlatives without data, and anything that reads like brochure copy.

## 8-Section Structure

### 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

One page. States what the document proves — usually 3 specific claims. Sets the tone as financial analysis, not marketing. Example framing: "This document proves three things: First, [X]. Second, [Y]. Third, [Z]."

### 2. THE COST OF INACTION

Quantify what the reader loses by not acting. Subsections typically include:
- Market data (population, searches, discovery behavior)
- Credibility/trust cost (consumer behavior statistics — how many check online first, how many judge by website)
- Mobile penalty (load time stats, bounce rates)
- Quantified loss in dollars (annual attributable revenue forfeited)

Every subsection needs at least one cited source.

### 3. WHAT THE SOLUTION DELIVERS

Lead generation math. Conversion rates by industry in table format. A conservative model (e.g., visitors × conversion rate × close rate = new customers/month). Cost-per-lead comparison: paid vs. organic.

### 4. COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE

What competitors are doing that the reader is missing. Describe the analysis framework (what dimensions are evaluated). This section establishes you did the homework — it's not generic advice.

### 5. YOUR ADVANTAGE

Differentiators with measurable claims. "48-72 hours" not "fast." "90+ PageSpeed" not "optimized." Each advantage gets a short subsection explaining why it matters to the bottom line, not just what it is.

### 6. PRICING & BREAK-EVEN MATH

All pricing fully disclosed. Break-even calculation: total cost ÷ average job value = jobs needed to break even. Then show: "at X jobs/month, site pays for itself in Y months." Include ROI multiplier for Year 1. Show the counterfactual (what paid ads cost for equivalent leads).

### 7. REAL NUMBERS TABLE

Industry-specific projections in table format. Columns: Industry, Avg Job Value, Leads/Mo, Close Rate, New Jobs/Mo, Monthly Revenue, Annual Revenue, Payback (months). At least 5-7 rows. All figures conservative and cited. Include methodology notes below the table.

### 8. NEXT STEPS

Numbered, concrete steps. Each step has a deliverable and a timeframe. Low-friction first step (audit, report, assessment). Clear upgrade path.

## Appendix Required

End with a sources table: Source, Year, Key Statistic. 10-15 cited sources minimum. Every claim in the body that uses data must trace to the appendix.

## Research Methodology

1. Multi-provider web search (Tavily for broad, Exa for deep/semantic, Serper/Brave for generic)
2. Prioritize industry-specific benchmarks over general business stats
3. Use local market data when available (metro population, regional pricing)
4. Cross-validate job values and conversion rates across at least 2 sources
5. Cite publisher + year in the appendix, not just the statistic

## Anti-Patterns

- Brochure language ("We're the best," "Revolutionary," "Game-changing")
- Vague claims without a source
- Unrealistic lead projections that undermine credibility
- Pricing buried or obscured — full disclosure builds trust
- Missing counterfactual (what happens if they do nothing)
